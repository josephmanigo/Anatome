## Common procedure and assessment rules

### Entry and phases

Complete the VR control orientation: select the skin, grip it, lift it by at least 0.04 m in the virtual scene, and release. This unlocks Easy activities. Reading the Guide does not complete orientation. Choose a mode, unit, difficulty, and phase: Learn step by step, Practice, or Assessment.

Lesson introduces the activity and demonstrations. Practice allows rehearsal and answer-revealing help. Assessment requires independent interaction; answer-revealing demonstrations are unavailable. The general Guide explains controls and pauses specimen interaction while open. There is no compulsory countdown and retries are unlimited.

Pass the current curriculum Easy assessment to unlock Medium in the same mode and unit. Pass Medium to unlock Hard. Lesson or Practice completion does not satisfy these assessment locks.

### How the game accepts an answer

The accepted response is a verified interaction with the required anatomy, tool, or decision model. Selecting a text answer or pressing Finish does not generate objective credit. Each action must match its target, action type, required instrument, equipment state, and applicable numeric thresholds. Required earlier objectives must already be complete.

Anatomy Cases and Guided Dissection use the listed sequence at every difficulty. Identify and Enumerate is ordered in Easy; Medium and Hard allow eligible selections in any order. Assemble Anatomy currently offers and accepts the current piece in sequence at every difficulty, even though its Medium and Hard catalog rubrics are marked unordered. Review this distinction when judging task design.

All 645 objectives are essential. An accepted duplicate observation of an already completed objective adds no credit and no ordinary mistake. Other submitted actions that do not match the current or eligible objective can add a mistake. Some incomplete gestures are filtered before submission, so a physical misalignment does not always produce a scoring event.

### Score and pass conditions

Score = max(0, 100 × completed objective weight / total objective weight − min(50, 5 × mistakes)).

A pass requires a score of at least 80, every essential objective complete, no critical failure, and no assisted flag. Weights are 1 for anatomy objectives and case finding selections; case procedure and decision objectives have weight 2. With every objective complete, four ordinary mistakes yield 80; five yield 75. A high score cannot override a critical failure.

A verified protected-structure violation causes a critical failure. Choosing Continue draining or Dismiss the symptom at the pleural-effusion safety decision also causes a critical failure when submitted as a valid dock interaction. Correcting the answer later does not remove that failure from the attempt.

Only completed, passed, unassisted assessments can unlock harder content and award new objective mastery rewards. The software rule is a proposed formative standard for professional review; it is not a clinically validated pass mark.

### Review questions about assessment design

## Mode procedures and difficulty settings

### Anatomy Cases

Inspect the finding on the specimen. Grip the mechanism model and release it into the patient-plan dock. Complete the authored instrument steps in order. Place the predicted response model in the dock, reassess the represented anatomy, and finish. In pleural effusion, complete the symptom-triggered safety decision before continuing. Hard and Assessment offer alternative instruments; successful choices must still satisfy the same objective.

### Identify and Enumerate

Read the requested anatomical name. Inspect the visible specimen, point at the correct structure, and press and release the trigger. Each accepted unique structure gains a marker. Easy contains the first three entries, Medium the first five, and Hard all eight in each unit. The required structures and their exact prompts are listed in the mode section.

### Assemble Anatomy

Grip the offered piece, move it into its anatomical relationship, rotate it, and release. Easy, Lesson, and Practice show a placement outline; Medium and Hard Assessments omit it. Accepted pieces return to the reference pose. Easy contains the first three entries, Medium the first five, and Hard all eight. Both position and rotation must meet the difficulty threshold.

### Guided Dissection

Work through every prepared station in the listed order. For a removal step, point at the named tissue, hold trigger and sweep a short cut, then release. Grip the detached chunk, lift it at least 0.06 m clear in the virtual scene, and release. Repeat until the removal meter is full. Whole layers stay anchored. For a preservation step, select and examine the named anatomy without cutting it. All difficulties cover the same stations.

Dissection automatically selects the bone saw for bone and the scalpel for other tissue. Named left or right regions use the specimen’s side; a chunk crossing the midline does not complete that regional requirement. Prepared openings and the removed-volume fractions are software teaching mechanisms, not clinical incision dimensions. Non-assessment hints show where to cut or lift but do not perform or score the action.

### Free dissection sandbox

Free dissection sandbox is part of Guided Dissection, not a fifth game mode. It has no authored questions, scored objectives, or difficulty variants. The normal assessment engine is absent, so sandbox work does not award assessment mastery.

Open Guided Dissection, then Free dissection sandbox. Explore the intact specimen, select a tissue and cutting tool, and make temporary cuts. Use Undo for eligible uncollected cuts or Restore specimen to reset the model. The workspace states: “Dissection sandbox · cuts are temporary. Use Undo or Restore specimen.”

Review whether the distinction between free exploration and a clinical procedure is clear, and whether the prepared specimen and protected anatomy are suitable for teaching.

## Anatomy unit teaching text

The following summaries and lessons are verbatim catalog text shared by Identify and Enumerate, Assemble Anatomy, and Guided Dissection. Protected structures apply to the corresponding unit in each mode.

### Chest wall

**Summary** Build a map of ribs, sternum and the protective thoracic cage.

**Lesson** The thoracic wall supports breathing and protects the organs beneath it. Begin at the manubrium, follow the body of the sternum and find its xiphoid process. Compare the first ribs with the lower ribs. Costal cartilage connects the anterior rib ends to the sternum in different patterns. Intercostal nerves and vessels need special attention when opening the chest wall. The removable model is a teaching aid; it does not represent a clinical incision plan.

**Preserve** Right intercostal nerve 4 [REC_Right_IC4_Nerve]

**Reference IDs** bodyparts

### Pleura and lungs

**Summary** Explore lung lobes, pleural layers and the diaphragm.

**Lesson** The right lung has three lobes and the left lung has two. Visceral pleura covers the lung; parietal pleura lines the thoracic cavity. The pleural space is normally a potential space between those surfaces. The diaphragm forms the floor of the thorax. Keep anatomical left and right anchored to the specimen, not your screen.

**Preserve** Right phrenic nerve [REC_Right_Phrenic_nerve]

**Reference IDs** bodyparts

### Airways

**Summary** Follow the trachea into the main bronchi and lobar branches.

**Lesson** Air passes from the trachea into the right and left main bronchi and then into branching airways. Trace each route in three dimensions. Airway narrowing can limit airflow without a large visible change to the outside of a lung. Close-up teaching views enlarge small structures and should not be mistaken for their life-size dimensions.

**Preserve** esophagus [FMA7131]

**Reference IDs** bodyparts

### Heart and coronary anatomy

**Summary** Connect the heart wall, coronary vessels and neighboring great vessels.

**Lesson** The coronary arteries supply the heart muscle. The left and right coronary arteries arise near the root of the aorta. Coronary veins return blood through routes including the coronary sinus. Distinguish a vessel carrying blood to the myocardium from a cardiac chamber containing blood. Cavity reference regions in this collection represent spaces, not pieces of myocardium.

**Preserve** Left phrenic nerve [REC_Left_Phrenic_nerve]; Right phrenic nerve [REC_Right_Phrenic_nerve]; right coronary artery [FMA50039]; left coronary artery [FMA50040]

**Reference IDs** bodyparts, heart-dissection, coronary-dissection

### Great vessels and valves

**Summary** Compare inflow, outflow and valve relationships.

**Lesson** The ascending aorta continues into the aortic arch and descending thoracic aorta. The pulmonary trunk divides toward the lungs. Pulmonary veins return toward the left atrium. Valve leaflets open and close in response to pressure differences. Their arrangement matters as much as their names. Separate components are enlarged or isolated only for teaching.

**Preserve** left coronary artery [FMA50040]

**Reference IDs** bodyparts

### Mediastinal relationships

**Summary** Find the esophagus, nerves and thoracic duct among the thoracic organs.

**Lesson** The mediastinum contains closely related organs, vessels, nerves and lymphatic structures. Follow the esophagus and compare its position with the trachea. Distinguish the phrenic nerves from the vagus nerves. Trace the thoracic duct through its regional course. Some fine structures in this collection are documented reconstructions; use the source panel to distinguish them.

**Preserve** left coronary artery [FMA50040]

**Reference IDs** bodyparts

## Complete shared Guide text

These ten pages are the in-game control and learning guide. Their text is reproduced verbatim. They are instructions, not scored questions.

### 1 Get comfortable

guide-welcome

Welcome to Anatome. I will guide you through the lab. Put on your headset and pick up both controllers. Look for the controller models and pointing rays. Open Menu, then Settings to choose seated or standing comfort and your dominant hand.

This guide stays available from the Guide button. Close guide returns you to the same screen. Your current activity waits while this guide is open.

### 2 Point and select

guide-point

Point a controller ray at a button. Squeeze the index trigger to activate a button immediately. Release it before pressing another button. Use the thumbstick while pointing at the panel to scroll. Hold the trigger on a slider and move the ray to adjust it.

Grip the top edge to move the panel. Menu also has Move panel in front of me. Release your controls before returning from a tracking interruption.

### 3 Hold move and release

guide-grip

Point at a movable structure or tool. Hold the side grip, move your hand, and turn your wrist. Release the grip to let go. A pointing trigger and a holding grip do different jobs.

Choose Try your first VR lesson, then Learn the VR controls. Select the skin, hold it, lift it, and release. Complete these physical actions to unlock Easy activities. Reading this guide does not complete them.

### 4 Choose your activity

guide-learn

Choose an activity, a unit, and a difficulty. Learn step by step introduces each action. Practice lets you rehearse. Assessment checks independent performance.

Follow the current step beside the specimen. In lessons and practice, Show me this step provides a demonstration. The Guide button explains controls without revealing assessment answers.

### 5 Identify and enumerate

guide-identify

Read the requested anatomical name. Inspect the three dimensional specimen, point at the structure, then squeeze and release the trigger. Mark each required structure in the set.

Use the specimen's own left and right. Coverings may be opened as a labelled teaching window. Hidden tissue is not missing anatomy; check the current activity and visible layers.

### 6 Assemble anatomy

guide-assemble

Grip the offered anatomical piece. Easy, lessons and practice show a placement outline. Move the piece into position, turn your wrist to match its orientation, then release. Medium and Hard assessments omit the outline; reconstruct the relationship from surrounding anatomy. Easy introduces one piece at a time.

More demanding levels require closer alignment. If it does not fit, compare the surrounding structures and try again. There is no compulsory countdown.

### 7 Cut and lift tissue sections

guide-dissect

Each topic has its own prepared anatomy stations. Easy, Medium and Hard cover the same anatomy, with less guidance and stricter removal requirements.

Point at the named tissue, hold trigger and sweep a short cut, then release. Grip the separated chunk, lift clear and release. The remaining tissue stays anchored. Follow the removal meter and complete both sides where named.

The bone saw equips automatically for ribs. Saw and scalpel buttons are above the instruction. Both tools reach the first surface within three metres. Losing the surface or pausing cancels the stroke until a fresh press. Preserve protected structures. Undo affects uncollected cuts; completed steps are saved.

### 8 Work through a case

guide-cases

Inspect the anatomical finding. For a mechanism or response question, grip a three dimensional model and release it inside the patient-plan dock. Read the feedback and reconsider the anatomical relationship.

For a procedure step, choose the indicated equipment, grip it, and use the trigger while completing its target, path or hold. Follow the case one step at a time. These fixed educational scenarios do not establish clinical competence.

### 9 Finish review and save

guide-progress

After the required steps, choose Finish activity or Finish assessment. Review the feedback and retry when you need more practice. Pass an assessment with at least eighty percent, every essential objective, and no critical safety failure.

Pass Easy to open Medium, then Medium to open Hard in that activity. Rewards record new unassisted mastery. Sign in to save progress; guest progress lasts only for this session.

### 10 Adjust or get help

guide-help

Menu, then Settings provides narration volume and speed, captions, text size and comfort controls. Pause or replay this guide's voice below. Close guide whenever you are ready, and open Guide again to continue here.

If a controller or ray disappears, check tracking and the controller battery, then release trigger and grip before trying again. Recenter the panel from Menu. If a problem persists, stop the activity and record the build version and step for your instructor.

This is a thorax teaching specimen with attributed reconstructions and reference regions. It does not contain the entire human body or every microscopic subdivision.
